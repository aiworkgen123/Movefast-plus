## Coverage rules (owner, 27 Sep 2026)

Approved by the owner on 27 September 2026. These decisions supersede the
25 September coverage rules below where they differ; existing recipes and live
planner behavior may still reflect earlier policy until their implementation is
updated.

### 1. Every placement carries an edit

- **STILL images:** use a small text label/caption, a template graphic, or a
  **VISIBLE** atmosphere filter (smoke/ash, snowfall, or film dust).
- **REAL FOOTAGE:** most of the time it can play as it is, but it always gets a
  visible filter (ash/dust etc.). Sometimes it is shown inside a frame instead
  of full screen, and sometimes it gets a text overlay.
- Zoom, grain, halftone, or vignette alone never count.

### 2. Text and template-graphic share

- **40–50% of placements** carry text or a template graphic. Text is mostly
  small labels and captions of 1–6 words: names, dates, places, and numbers,
  such as “RUDOLF DIESEL · 1858” or “38,000 MEN”.
- Big P01/P02 headlines are rare, about 1 in 6 texted placements, and are
  2–8 words.
- The P03 word-highlight card is the exception.
- Never invent facts.

### 3. Still-image motion

Stills are mostly static. Use a gentle zoom or pan on only about 1 in 4 stills.

### 4. CTA and structure beats

- Any narration asking to subscribe/like/comment/bell/notification becomes its
  own beat, with a picture or footage that fits the neighbouring beats plus a
  SUBSCRIBE (or similar) overlay.
- Structure beats (“Now, board one.”, “Three boards tonight”) get a fitting
  picture plus a bold, centred, all-caps title (chapter card).

### 5. No black frames

Never use black. A gap uses fitting media from the neighbouring beats’ world,
plus text if useful; it is never a plain card on black.

### 6. Template graphics

Use a template graphic where it genuinely fits: WC06 chess animations
(knight_fork, back_rank_mate, or queen_capture); the 2–4 boards graphic; WC08
identity/place labels; WC12 person introduction when a person first appears; or
WC09/P14 for key numbers and years.

## Coverage rules (owner, 25 Sep 2026)

Historical owner rules approved on 25 September 2026. The current 27 September
rules above supersede this section where they differ. These rules replaced the
earlier line "ordinary imagery is a complete editorial choice".

## 1. Every placement carries an edit
Every moment on screen has at least one edit. A plain photo or clip, with or without zoom or pan, is never enough.

## 2. What counts as an edit (strongest first)
a. **Template graphic:** any available War Chess specialist, for example person introduction (WC12), map orientation (WC04), two-force comparison (WC01), chess analogy (WC06), statistic (WC09), identity and place (WC08), document reveal (WC13), three-subject sequence (WC07), anchored progression (WC02), archival detail (WC14), two-source contrast (WC15), year anchor (P14).
b. **Text overlay** on the image or on a clip:
   - Headlines (P01, P02) are **short and punchy: 2–8 words**, faithful to the narration.
   - The **word-highlight card (P03)** is the exception: a longer line with 1–2 words highlighted.
c. **Full-screen image or clip with a visible atmosphere filter:** smoke/ash, snowfall or film dust. It must be clearly visible at normal viewing size.

**Not an edit on its own:** zoom, pan, film grain, halftone, vignette.

## 3. Choice order for each placement
Use (a) when its facts and media exist. Otherwise use (b). Otherwise use (c).
Never invent a fact, name, date, number or quote to fill a slot.

## 4. Mix
At least **half** of all placements use (a) or (b). Filter-only (c) covers at most half.

## 5. Repetition
The same kind of edit several times in a row is fine when there is no better option (for example three person introductions for Rommel, Patton and Montgomery). Otherwise vary it. Never repeat the same headline idea.

## 6. Pacing
Each tool keeps its template duration limits (text overlays 3–5 seconds). A long beat gets several edits in sequence.

## 7. Report
The final run report lists every placement with its edit, source and cost.

## How to choose

Choose the visual that answers the viewer's main question in the current narration. Read the supplied story context; a mentioned name, date, number, or object does not automatically require its matching template. Template roles are useful defaults, and sensible overlap is allowed: resolve it by what this beat needs to explain. Under the 25 September coverage rules, ordinary imagery alone is incomplete: every placement needs a graphic, text, or visible atmosphere filter. Only the supplied selectable templates and map modes are available for this request.

Prefer variety across a passage, but allow repeated or adjacent treatments when they support continuity, comparison, a developing argument, or a new emphasis. There is no per-section quota or mandatory alternation. In the component's reason, explain its contribution to this beat; for a repeat, explain what develops or why continuity helps. Avoid repeatedly announcing the same point merely for decoration. 

Let narration, comprehension, and reading time guide pacing within the supplied rendering limits; do not stretch the voice-over or force a fixed cutting rhythm. Media requirements describe what sourcing must obtain and review, not assets you may pretend are already present. If a needed fact is absent, choose an honest alternative.

## What stays firm

- Preserve the script, voice-over, factual qualifications, and frame ownership.
- Historical claims and identities still require their existing evidence checks; keep media provenance. Rights checks and flags are superseded by the 25 September owner decision.
- Text must fit its design; simultaneous full-screen treatments cannot cover the same frames.
- A design must have the required reviewed assets and fit its current renderer. These technical limits do not impose a cutting rhythm.

## Template recipes

### Phrase emphasis (P01)

Land one short takeaway, reveal, consequence, or turning point.

**Choose:** Use when the whole phrase deserves attention. P03 emphasizes individual words; P02 develops a complete editorial statement. A routine sentence need not become a title.

**Fill:** The 25 September owner target is a faithful two-to-eight-word headline. The earlier live recipe here used two to seven words; implementation must be updated separately. Any emphasis must be an exact phrase from that headline. Choose an honest reason and coordination role; a hook or takeaway role must have its named neighboring specialist.

**Media:** Use a reviewed still of this beat's subject or direct context, with clear space for the phrase.

**Current rendering capacity:** 3–5 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Editorial statement (P02)

Give a complete thesis, reframe, or conclusion room to register.

**Choose:** Use when the sentence itself is the editorial point. P01 is a shorter takeaway and P03 centers one or two words. Prefer a specialist when its structure explains the point better.

**Fill:** The 25 September owner target is a short, punchy, faithful two-to-eight-word statement. The earlier live recipe here used five to twelve words; implementation must be updated separately. Choose its editorial purpose and describe the actual subject or direct context needed beneath it.

**Media:** Use a reviewed still that supports the same thought, with a legible text area rather than generic mood imagery.

**Current rendering capacity:** 4–5 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Word emphasis (P03)

Make one or two consequential words stand out within a short phrase.

**Choose:** Use when the wording carries a distinction, reversal, or emphasis. P01 gives the complete phrase equal importance; P02 presents a complete editorial thought.

**Fill:** The 25 September owner target permits a longer faithful line with one or two words highlighted. The earlier live recipe here used a two-to-seven-word headline; implementation must be updated separately. Request a clear placement; final image framing must protect faces and evidence.

**Media:** Use a reviewed still of the subject or direct context, with enough clear space for readable emphasis.

**Current rendering capacity:** 3–5 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Year anchor (P14)

Orient the viewer to a meaningful year or change in time.

**Choose:** Use when the year is the beat's main point. A passing date need not interrupt the edit. Prefer WC12 when introducing the person is the actual job.

**Fill:** Use exactly one four-digit year spoken in this beat and choose the truthful selection basis. Do not import a year merely because another source mentions it.

**Media:** Use a reviewed still of the same subject or direct context; the year and person treatment must not compete in the same frames.

**Current rendering capacity:** 3–5 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Two-force comparison (WC01)

Help the viewer distinguish two forces or sides.

**Choose:** Use when the relationship between two forces matters. WC07 presents three subjects; WC15 compares two source documents or images rather than two military identities.

**Fill:** Supply exactly two distinct force ids with concise labels and only supported claim references. Do not invent force sizes, capabilities, or an equality the narration does not establish.

**Media:** Obtain two separately reviewed identity assets, one for each force, with consistent and honest representation.

**Current rendering capacity:** 5 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Anchored progression (WC02)

Explain several related points through one stable subject or anchor.

**Choose:** Use for a meaningful progression, contrast, cause-and-effect relationship, or set of facets around one person, place, object, document, group, or symbol. An unrelated list does not qualify; WC07 is useful for a three-subject overview.

**Fill:** Name the shared anchor and explicit relationship, then supply two to five distinct related points with short labels and details. Preserve uncertainty: sequence alone does not prove causation.

**Media:** The production route needs a reviewed anchor background, a suitable cutout, and a separately reviewed image for every point. Allow enough narration for the progression to be understood. Admission requires at least max(240, 90 + 60 × point_count) frames: two, three, four, and five points need at least 8, 9, 11, and 13 seconds respectively at 30 fps, within the 20-second maximum.

**Current rendering capacity:** 8–20 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Map orientation (WC04)

Show where a named place or region is in relation to the story.

**Choose:** Use when geography helps comprehension. A location name alone may need only WC08 or ordinary imagery. The current production map supports orientation, not tactical positions or movement.

**Fill:** Use verified claim ids and orientation mode. Leave actors, movement_steps, static_relationship, and directional_arrows null. Do not infer routes, distances, direction, or troop positions from narration alone.

**Media:** Obtain a reviewed map plate of the correct place and relevant geography. Missing map evidence calls for an honest alternative, not a fabricated map.

**Current rendering capacity:** 5–15 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Chess analogy (WC06)

Make a strategic relationship easier to grasp through a concrete chess idea.

**Choose:** Use when a supplied legal motif illuminates the current military idea. Chess-themed wording alone is not enough. The game illustrates an analogy; it does not prove the historical tactic or turn history into a literal chess position.

**Fill:** Choose only a supplied motif_id and explain the correspondence in the component reason. Never supply a FEN, move line, evaluation, engine claim, or authority wording. If no admitted motif fits, use another treatment.

**Media:** The code-owned board and verified engine plan provide the visual. No stock chess image or invented board state is needed.

**Current rendering capacity:** 8–20 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Three-subject sequence (WC07)

Introduce or distinguish three subjects as a coherent group.

**Choose:** Use when understanding all three subjects together helps the story. Prefer WC02 when one anchor and an explicit relationship organize a developing explanation.

**Fill:** Supply exactly three distinct subjects with concise labels and supported references. Their order should follow the explanation without inventing a ranking or chronology.

**Media:** Obtain three separately reviewed subject assets with comparable clarity and honest identities.

**Current rendering capacity:** 5–15 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Identity and place (WC08)

Quickly establish who or what is being shown and where it belongs.

**Choose:** Use for concise orientation over an existing scene. WC12 gives a person a dedicated introduction; WC04 explains actual geography.

**Fill:** Provide the supported identity and place as concise labels. Do not imply the base image depicts an exact location unless that identity is established.

**Media:** Use the already reviewed base scene when it honestly supports the labels and leaves them readable.

**Current rendering capacity:** 3–5 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Verified statistic (WC09)

Make a consequential quantity or scale easy to understand.

**Choose:** Use when the number changes the viewer's understanding, not for every number mentioned. P14 is specifically for a spoken year.

**Fill:** Supply the statistic, its unit, and a verification-eligible claim id. Preserve qualifiers, units, and uncertainty; do not add precision or independently calculate a new historical claim.

**Media:** Use a reviewed base scene that gives the statistic honest context without suggesting unsupported visual proof.

**Current rendering capacity:** 3–5 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Strategic choice (WC10)

Explain a real decision between alternatives and which option was taken.

**Choose:** Use when the decision is the current beat's point. An outcome by itself is not a choice, and alternatives must not be invented to fill the design.

**Fill:** Supply two to four supported options with unique ids, concise labels, and eligible references. chosen_option_id must match the documented choice. Decorative cartography is not geographic evidence.

**Media:** The design supplies an abstract decision surface. Its production evidence connection is still pending; it is not currently a selectable live treatment.

**Current rendering capacity:** 5–15 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Chronology (WC11)

Make the supported order of events clear.

**Choose:** Use when event order itself explains the beat. WC02 is better suited to facets or relationships around a shared anchor; a sequence must not imply unsupported causation.

**Fill:** Supply two to six supported events in order, with unique ids and concise labels. The renderer adds sequence numbers, not dates or proportional intervals. Do not add future events or invent dates.

**Media:** The design supplies an abstract timeline. Its production evidence connection is still pending; it is not currently a selectable live treatment.

**Current rendering capacity:** 5–15 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Person introduction (WC12)

Help the viewer recognize a person and understand their role in this moment.

**Choose:** Use for a meaningful introduction or reintroduction after the story changes context. A repeated name alone need not trigger it. WC08 is lighter identity/place orientation; P14 is for a year that matters more than a new introduction.

**Fill:** Give the supported name and concise role appropriate to the period being discussed. Do not invent ranks, titles, or later positions.

**Media:** Obtain a reviewed portrait and matching cutout of the correct person, with usable silhouette and framing. The current renderer requires its reviewed five-second base scene.

**Current rendering capacity:** A reviewed five-second base scene in the current production route.

### Document evidence reveal (WC13)

Direct attention to a specific piece of documentary evidence.

**Choose:** Use when a detail in a document supports the point being made. WC14 explores several regions within one visual source; WC15 compares two sources.

**Fill:** Identify the document, supported date, relevant detail, and eligible claim references. A highlight must point to material actually present; do not invent a quotation, signature, or date.

**Media:** Obtain a reviewed full document and a reviewed detail from that same document, with enough resolution to make the evidence visible.

**Current rendering capacity:** 5–10 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Archival detail sequence (WC14)

Guide the viewer through several meaningful details within one source.

**Choose:** Use when exploring a painting, photograph, map, or artifact reveals useful information. WC13 focuses a documentary proof point; WC02 explains relationships across separately illustrated points.

**Fill:** Identify one source and two to four distinct visible details, each with a concise label and supported references. Sequence them to serve the narration without turning visual order into a false historical sequence.

**Media:** Obtain a reviewed full source with sufficient resolution and separately reviewed regions belonging to that same source.

**Current rendering capacity:** 5–10 seconds at the current 30 fps.

### Two-source contrast (WC15)

Let two identified sources clarify a real contrast.

**Choose:** Use for a supported plan/outcome, claim/consequence, advance/retreat, or competing-account comparison. WC01 distinguishes forces; WC14 examines one source in detail.

**Fill:** Supply exactly two independently identified sources, their kinds and supported date labels, and the actual relationship being compared. Explain what differs without overstating what the sources prove.

**Media:** Obtain two separately reviewed sources and preserve their identities. Do not merge them into a false composite or imply that unrelated images document the same event.

**Current rendering capacity:** 5–10 seconds at the current 30 fps.

## Sourcing: practical yes/no principles

- Yes: keep ranked, searchable visible subjects, actions and reactions. One honest anchor can carry a shot; alternatives should add a useful choice.
- Yes: search the chosen anchor with concrete subject/action and necessary context. A player reaction remains a reaction, even when the primary anchor is an opening move.
- Yes: use contemporary physical chess as clearly illustrative B-roll. An opening setup and first move can illustrate a blunder without proving the move was bad or showing a six-week diagram. A restrained reaction may illustrate realisation without asserting immediate discovery.
- No: require one image to contain every narrated detail. A verified Patton portrait need not also show Sicily; a chosen Sicily place shot need not contain Patton. A wrong person presented as Patton still fails.
- Yes: let the rejection cause guide a bounded retry: broaden for scarcity, strengthen subject/action for drift, restore declared era for wrong-era evidence, or change approved visual form for text dominance or repetition. The final new contextual query is one budgeted editorial decision.
- No: rotate words, invent facts, drop protected subjects, add generic historical labels to explicit illustration, or accept an unrelated shot. Observed midgame, branded digital gameplay and talking-presenter footage do not satisfy an opening-move shot.
- Yes: reuse previously found local project media automatically. Uploads remain optional. Motion, provenance, exact claims and uncertainty checks remain required. The earlier rights check is superseded by the 25 September owner decision.
